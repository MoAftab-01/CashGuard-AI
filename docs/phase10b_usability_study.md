# Phase 10B: repeatable first-user usability study

Prepared October 20, 2026. **No participants have been tested and no results are claimed.**
Use only the bundled fictional transactions. This study evaluates understanding and
navigation, not financial outcomes, model accuracy or accessibility conformance.

## Setup and participant instructions

Recruit 3–5 nontechnical volunteers, including someone who uses keyboard navigation
if willing. Allow 15 minutes per participant. Do not collect financial histories,
account details or identifying information. Use an anonymous participant ID.
Ask permission before any recording; handwritten observations are sufficient.

Open a fresh browser session on the synthetic-only demo. Confirm the public-demo
banner and absence of an upload widget. Select **Start demo**, with fictional opening
cash 2,500.00, USD display label and minimum threshold 500.00. Do not change these
assumptions between participants. Clear the previous session; do not reuse their plan.
Use the same viewport and release commit for a comparable round. Record assistive
technology, viewport and input method, without collecting medical information.

Read aloud:

> This is a prototype using fictional money and transactions. We are testing the
> product, not you. Please say what you are looking for and what you expect to happen.
> You can stop at any time. These are estimates and hypothetical plans, not bank
> balances or financial advice. I will mostly observe; tell me when you are done.

Give one task at a time using only the **participant task** wording below. Do not
name the navigation destination or reveal the success criterion beforehand.

## Tasks and objective criteria

| # | Participant task | Unassisted success criterion | Time limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find how much cash you have now. Tell me what this number means. | Finds Home's estimated cash balance and explains it is reconstructed from fictional transactions plus opening cash, not a verified bank balance. | 90 s |
| 2 | Find whether an upcoming cash-flow problem is expected. Explain what could make the answer change. | Finds the 30-day forecast, compares the threshold and breach dates, and describes the projection as conditional. Correctly saying the default sample has no breach is success; inventing a problem is not. | 150 s |
| 3 | Explore what would happen if you faced an unexpected expense. Explain what changed and what stayed the same. | Uses Plan → Unexpected expense (or enters 500), sees closing balance change −500.00, distinguishes future simulation from unchanged historical cash. | 150 s |
| 4 | Find the unusual-activity review and explain what an alert would mean. | Finds Unusual Activity, distinguishes unusual from confirmed fraud and not-flagged from unscored. If the default data has no alert, recognizes this and locates the review-method explanation. Does not claim zero risk. | 150 s |
| 5 | Find the assistant and tell me whether its current answer was generated by an AI model. | Finds AI Assistant; correctly identifies the deterministic calculated summary in the public demo and the stated provider boundary. | 90 s |

For task 4, a separate optional local-mode session may use the existing, labeled
[synthetic anomaly fixture](submission/assets/synthetic-anomaly-benchmark.csv).
Record this as a **different study condition**, reset the other inputs, and ask the
participant to find an actual flag and its historical-comparison explanation. Do
not enable uploads on the public instance or present this as an ordinary user's data.

Do not correct a participant's answer before timing stops. At the limit, record
failure or partial completion, then offer one neutral prompt: “What else might you
try?” Mark all subsequent completion **assisted**. Stop if the participant wishes.

Use one stopwatch. Start after reading each task, stop when the participant declares
completion or reaches the limit. Include thinking time. Record interruptions
separately; do not subtract them silently. Never turn assisted success into
unassisted success. Severity: blocker / major confusion / minor friction / suggestion.

## Feedback and reporting

After each task ask: “How easy was that, from 1 (very difficult) to 7 (very easy)?”
Then ask:

1. Which number or explanation did you trust least, and why?
2. What did you expect the cash forecast to know that it did not?
3. Could you tell which inputs changed your plan?
4. What does an unusual transaction mean to you?
5. What do you think stays on this computer, and what reaches the server or model?
6. What would you change before using a tool like this?

Report per-task counts and median elapsed times with the actual participant count,
assisted completions, failures and range. Include anonymized observations, not
invented testimonials. A small convenience sample is formative evidence; it cannot
establish population-wide usability, financial benefit or full accessibility.

Proposed go/no-go criterion for another formative round: no participant mistakes an
estimated balance for bank-verified cash, an unusual flag for fraud, or a calculated
summary for live AI output; investigate any such misunderstanding before public
promotion. This is a proposed criterion, not a passed gate.
